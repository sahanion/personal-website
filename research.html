Nano Covalent Organic Frameworks for Targeted Cancer Therapy

Covalent Organic Frameworks (COFs) represent an emerging class of crystalline porous polymers offering modular pore architectures and immense internal surface areas. This research investigates the rational surface chemistry engineering of sub-100 nm COF nanoparticles to overcome biological barriers, achieve high payload stabilization, and execute triggered release inside cancer cells.

  • Engineered Surface Functionalization: Tailored surface functional groups on nano-COFs to govern protein corona formation, receptor recognition, and cellular internalization efficiency.
  • Triggered Payload Delivery: Optimized antineoplastic drug loading inside pore cavities, achieving a 50% enhancement in targeted cancer cell lethality over free therapeutic molecules.
  • Multimodal Physicochemical Characterization: Validated crystalline integrity, morphology, and monodispersity via FE-SEM, HR-TEM, DLS, and Powder X-Ray Diffraction (PXRD).
  • Subcellular Fate Tracking: Visualized endocytic pathways and intracellular localization across distinct organelle compartments using Confocal Laser Scanning Microscopy (CLSM).

Nanofiber-Supported Covalent Organic Frameworks for Molecular Sorption

While powder-form Covalent Organic Frameworks exhibit remarkable intrinsic porosity, their real-world separation and sorption utility is often hampered by particle agglomeration and recovery hurdles. In this work, we developed an electrospinning methodology to immobilize nano/mesoscale COF crystallites across a continuous polymeric nanofiber matrix for ultra-high-efficiency volatile iodine capture.

  • Electrospun Nanofiber Matrix: Synthesized porous hybrid nanofiber webs providing mechanical robustness and open gas-permeable transport channels.
  • Enhanced Iodine Sorption: Maximized active reticular sorption sites through homogeneous COF dispersion, achieving unprecedented sorption capacities and rapid uptake kinetics.
  • Recyclability & Environmental Safety: Demonstrated structural stability across consecutive sorption-desorption cycles without framework collapse or leaching.

Electrochemical Point-of-Care Diagnostic Sensor Strips

Bridging nanomaterial surface chemistry with functional clinical diagnostics. This research focused on formulating biopolymer-stabilized sensing interfaces on microfabricated electrode strips, enabling rapid, enzyme-mediated electrochemical detection for metabolic point-of-care monitoring.

  • Biopolymer Nanocomposite Formulation: Engineered cross-linked biopolymer matrices ensuring high enzyme retention, low nonspecific fouling, and rapid electron transfer kinetics.
  • Microelectrode Fabrication: Evaluated multi-electrode configurations (working, reference, counter) for micro-volume sample wetting and reproducible redox signaling.
  • Data Analytics & Calibration Modeling: Implemented analytical workflows and data processing pipelines to calibrate sensor currents against physiological concentration gradients.

Inorganic Carbon Assimilation & Photobioreactor Engineering

Explored closed-loop biological remediation by cultivating indigenous microalgae (Desmodesmas intermedius) isolated from industrial effluents. The project combined custom low-cost photobioreactor fabrication, photon-path optimization, and downstream metabolic profiling to maximize carbon sequestration and lipid/protein accumulation.

  • Photobioreactor Design: Built and commissioned a low-cost, scalable photobioreactor with optimized gas sparging for enhanced dissolved CO₂ mass transfer.
  • Microbial Strain Optimization: Isolated effluent-tolerant microalgae and engineered balanced nutrient formulations to stimulate rapid vegetative growth.
  • Biochemical Profiling: Conducted quantitative spectrophotometric and chromatographic assays to characterize total lipids, proteins, and carbohydrate yields.
